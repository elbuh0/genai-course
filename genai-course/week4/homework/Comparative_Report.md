Comparative Evaluation: Report Product Ad vs Photo-Realism
 
No single configuration performed best across all prompts; the winner depended on the prompt type. SDXL Turbo (Config C) produced my preferred output where realism mattered to me, i.e., the interior, the drone-surveilled tank, and the muscle car, while SDXL base (Config A) won the bee and SDXL + Canny (Config B) the watch.

Configs A and B were strongest where the prompt called for brilliance. Config A’s bee was overall best, with vibrant colors and apparent anatomical accuracy. Its slightly matte flower surface also appeared more realistic to me, communicating a more organic, skin-like structure than Config B’s dew-like brilliance, as if photographed early morning -a close contest, emphasizing the role of user preference. For the watch, both showed good structural accuracy despite hand, text placement, and bezel marking errors. However, I preferred the catalogue-quality brilliance of Config B, which better aligned with a Swiss luxury sports watch. Config A’s matte bracelet lacked that jewel-like brilliance and might better suit a worn vintage pilot’s watch advertisement. Otherwise A and B were largely equal.

Config C was strongest where realism mattered more than shine. Though lacking catalogue-type light reflectivity, with somewhat muted or dusty colors, I preferred the realism of its interior. Its Mustang’s proportions were also far more accurately rendered, whereas A and B slightly exaggerated the grille height, losing realism to a more stereotypically “muscle car” look. On the ISR tank, C was the default winner as the only properly rendered tank, with interestingly rendered camouflage; A and B were unusable due to a nonsensical second main gun and its placement. No configuration rendered the anti-drone caging, perhaps due to minimal training examples, as such defensive measures are relatively new battlefield developments.
